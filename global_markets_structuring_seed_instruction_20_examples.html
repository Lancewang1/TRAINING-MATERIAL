Part I

Seed-writing instructions

Designed to parallel the equity-research seed instruction while accounting for contractual path dependence, private OTC evidence, market-data conventions, and model-dependent valuation.

1. Purpose

Write realistic Global Markets structuring and exotic-derivatives research requests that a structurer, trader, quantitative analyst, salesperson, or markets analyst might genuinely need to solve.

These are seed tasks, not normally the final training examples. A downstream Mint-style generator should abstract away the original issuer, instrument, dates, and numerical values; preserve the reasoning structure; locate a new real evidence pack; construct and replay a hidden answer graph; and only then generate the final agentic question.

The contributor's most valuable output is the reusable reasoning pattern. The final example should remain useful after company/instrument names, dates, strikes, barriers, rates, and market levels are replaced.

2. What Makes a Good Seed

  • Real work: contractual interpretation, fixing/settlement analysis, payoff reconstruction, path-dependent logic, market normalization, or cross-source comparison.
  • Multiple evidence pieces: terms + market observations; several observation dates; multiple underlyings; benchmark methodology + fixing; or multiple payoff inputs.
  • Meaningful reasoning chain: find/inspect terms → align market data → apply conventions → derive intermediate values → evaluate conditions → calculate/compare → answer.
  • Unique and replayable: once the evidence and conventions are fixed, there should be one objectively defensible result.
  • Reusable: removing names/dates/numbers should leave a useful workflow.

3. Public Structured Securities vs. Private OTC

Do not assume bespoke bilateral OTC confirmations are web-searchable. For private OTC tasks, the confirmation, term sheet, trade record, or internal product document should be supplied to the agent as an authorized private artifact. The agent may then retrieve historical fixings, curves, prices, or benchmark observations through approved market-data tools.

Registered structured notes are different: SEC 424B2 pricing supplements often expose detailed payoff terms publicly and are useful source anchors for public-data training.

Evidence-access pattern can differ by domain.
Equity research often starts with question → public research.
Exotics often starts with question + private/public contract artifact → market-data research → payoff reasoning.

4. Contractual vs. Model-Dependent Questions

Prefer contractual or realized-outcome tasks. A question such as “Did the note autocall, and what was paid?” can be replayed from terms and historical observations.

Model-dependent questions such as “What is the fair value of this autocallable?” are acceptable only when the valuation date, pricing model, curves, volatility surface, correlation, dividends, calendars, conventions, and numerical tolerances are sufficiently fixed. Otherwise there is no unique ground truth.

5. Difficulty Should Come from Finance

Useful difficulty includes date/fixing alignment, quote direction, barrier-monitoring frequency, worst-of selection, memory coupons, knock-in/knock-out state, non-call periods, day-count conventions, caps/floors, basket weights, step-down thresholds, and distinguishing preliminary from final terms.

Avoid artificial arithmetic or obscure wording merely to make a task longer.

6. Questions to Avoid

  • “Is this note attractive?” or “Which structure is better?” without a fixed objective.
  • Unspecified exotic pricing or hedging questions.
  • Tasks requiring private dealer marks or proprietary surfaces that the environment does not provide.
  • Continuous-barrier questions when only daily-close data is available.
  • Questions that expose the full search route or exact URLs in the user-facing task.
  • Tasks asking the model to predict a discretionary issuer call as though it were a mechanical condition.

Worst-of Auto-Call: First Call Date, Coupons, and Total Cash Paid

Equity / Structured NotesWorst-of contingent-income autocallHardHistorical candidate
⌄
REAL PUBLIC SOURCE ANCHOR
Morgan Stanley — Contingent Income Auto-Callable Securities due May 1, 2024 (AAPL / AMZN / TSLA)

Public SEC pricing supplement. The note is linked to the worst performing of Apple, Amazon, and Tesla, has quarterly observation dates, a one-year initial non-call period, contingent coupons, automatic early redemption, and a downside threshold at maturity.

Open public source ↗
SEED QUESTION
Using the contractual observation schedule and the historical closing prices of all three underlying stocks, determine which contingent quarterly coupons were earned, whether the securities were automatically redeemed before maturity, the first redemption date if applicable, and the total cash paid per $1,000 stated principal amount through termination.
EXPECTED ANSWER

Coupon count + yes/no + date + monetary value

WHY UNIQUE / VERIFIABLE

The observation dates, thresholds, closing prices, and contractual cash-flow rules uniquely determine the path.

REQUIRED EVIDENCE
  • Final pricing supplement and observation/redemption schedule
  • Initial prices and coupon/downside thresholds for all three stocks
  • Historical closing prices on every relevant observation date
  • Coupon amount and early-redemption payment terms
PROPOSED REASONING TRACE
  1. Extract the non-call period, coupon condition, auto-call condition, thresholds, and payment schedule.
  2. For each observation date, retrieve the closing price of every underlying and normalize each against its contractual initial price.
  3. Test the coupon condition using the worst-performing/threshold logic.
  4. Starting only when the note becomes callable, test the automatic-redemption condition chronologically.
  5. Stop at the first qualifying call date; otherwise continue through the final observation date.
  6. Aggregate coupons actually due and add the applicable redemption or maturity payment.
  7. Return coupon count, first call date (if any), termination state, and total cash paid.

Memory Coupon Catch-Up and Auto-Call

Equity / Structured NotesSingle-stock autocall with memory interestHardSource anchor — future-dated observations

REAL PUBLIC SOURCE ANCHOR
JPMorgan — Auto Callable Contingent Interest Notes linked to AMD due July 13, 2027

Public SEC pricing supplement. The note pays monthly contingent interest when AMD is at or above 50% of initial value, carries forward previously unpaid coupons, and becomes automatically callable from the third review date when AMD is at or above its initial value.

Open public source ↗
SEED QUESTION
Using the note's review-date schedule and historical AMD closes, reconstruct the contingent-interest cash flows through the specified cutoff, identify any missed coupons that were later recovered under the memory feature, and determine whether and when the note was automatically called.
EXPECTED ANSWER

Cash-flow schedule + yes/no + date + total payment

WHY UNIQUE / VERIFIABLE

The memory balance is mechanically determined by the sequence of review-date closes and the fixed contract rules.

REQUIRED EVIDENCE
  • Initial AMD value and 50% interest barrier
  • Monthly contingent-interest amount
  • Review dates and earliest callable review date
  • Historical AMD closing prices on each review date
  • Memory-interest and automatic-call terms
PROPOSED REASONING TRACE
  1. Extract the interest barrier, monthly coupon, memory rule, call threshold, and call-eligibility start date.
  2. Walk review dates in chronological order.
  3. At each date, test whether the interest barrier is met; if not, add that coupon to an unpaid-coupon balance.
  4. When the barrier is next met, pay the current coupon plus any accumulated unpaid coupons and reset the balance.
  5. On callable dates, separately test whether the closing price is at or above the initial value.
  6. If the call condition is met, add principal plus current/memory coupons and terminate the path.
  7. Report the missed-coupon periods, catch-up payment(s), and call outcome.

REQUIRED EVIDENCE

  • Strike and barrier exchange rate
  • Leveraged exchange factor
  • Maximum and minimum payments
  • Exact below-strike payoff formula
PROPOSED REASONING TRACE
  1. Compare the final exchange rate with the strike.
  2. If at/above strike, select the maximum-payment branch.
  3. If below strike, calculate (1/final FX) minus (1/strike).
  4. Multiply by the leveraged exchange factor and $1,000, then subtract from maximum payment.
  5. Apply the contractual minimum-payment floor.
  6. Convert final payment into total return.

Method notes

Public-data scope. The examples deliberately use SEC-filed registered structured securities because detailed economics can be public. Bespoke bilateral OTC confirmations are often private and should be supplied as authorized artifacts rather than assumed discoverable on the open web.

Reasoning trace. The listed traces are task specifications / required operations, not hidden model chain-of-thought. They are intended to be converted into structured seed abstractions and evidence DAGs.

Historical verification. Some recent filings have future observation or maturity dates and are marked as source anchors. For training-example construction, choose a historical instance with all required market observations available before generating the final question.

Source provenance. Each worked example links to the public SEC source used to anchor the product structure. Market observations should be obtained from the fixing source required by the contract, an official exchange/benchmark source, or a licensed dataset available to the training environment.
